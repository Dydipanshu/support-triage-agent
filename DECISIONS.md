# Decision log

Non-obvious decisions, in the order they were made.

1. **Brand: Delta.** Third-largest support volume in the dataset (23,608 first-contact threads) but with a bounded domain. Amazon and Apple have more volume but their intents sprawl across thousands of products. Delta's escalation boundary is also unusually clean: agents move almost anything touching a booking to DM.
2. **Unit of evaluation is the first customer tweet and Delta's first reply.** 70 percent of Delta threads are exactly two turns. Multi-turn handling would have doubled the work for the minority case, and the first reply is where an auto-responder would actually run.
3. **Delta's split replies ("1/2", "2/2") are joined into one reply, ordered by tweet id.** Without this, retrieval shows half a reply and the agent learns to write fragments. Agent signatures like "*TBT" are stripped so the model does not imitate them.
4. **Intents describe what the customer needs Delta to do, not the topic.** "Seat" appears in praise, policy questions, and change requests. Action-oriented labels make the escalation decision follow from the intent.
5. **Nine intents, not more.** A 300-message read produced 14 candidate buckets. Buckets with under 3 percent of traffic were merged into their nearest actionable neighbour. Rare intents cannot be measured on a 220-example set anyway.
6. **"Escalate" means a human reads the tweet before the public reply is posted, nothing more.** Under this definition most delays, complaints, and bag problems are auto: the safe reply is an apology plus a DM request, which is what Delta posts. Defining escalation as "needs a human at some point" would make it true for almost everything and useless as a metric.
7. **Six named escalation reasons, and the golden set carries the reason.** This makes escalation recall diagnosable per reason instead of a single number.
8. **Golden set stratified by a cheap weak label, then hand-labelled from scratch.** Random sampling gives about 40 percent praise and under 4 percent baggage. The weak label from `gpt-4.1-nano` is only used to pick which messages to read; it is deleted before labelling.
9. **Golden threads are removed from the retrieval index.** Otherwise the agent retrieves the exact historical reply for a golden message and the groundedness score measures leakage.
10. **Rules form an escalation floor under the LLM.** Compensation, PII, legal or medical keywords, and hostile language force escalation even when the model says auto. The model can only add escalations, never remove one. This is the one place where a false positive is cheap and a false negative is expensive.
11. **The judge is a different model family from the generator.** `gpt-5.6-sol` judges `gpt-4.1-mini` output. Same-model judging is known to inflate scores through self-preference.
12. **The judge sees Delta's real reply as a reference, but is told it may be imperfect.** Without a reference the judge rewards fluent replies that ignore how Delta actually handles the case. With it treated as ground truth, the judge penalises replies that are better than Delta's.
13. **Every LLM call is cached on disk by content hash.** The 15-minute reproduction target is only met because a second run makes zero API calls. Deleting `data/cache/` reruns everything for roughly 3 USD.
14. **Cheapest capable models throughout.** `gpt-4.1-mini` for the agent (no reasoning tokens, temperature 0), `text-embedding-3-small` for retrieval, `gpt-4.1-nano` for weak labels. The judge is the only expensive call and runs at low reasoning effort.
15. **The simple baseline trains on weak labels, not golden labels.** Training on golden and testing on golden would be leakage. Training on 1,780 weak labels is the honest cheap option and is also what a team without a golden set would do.
16. **After the first full run, one rule was added: `other` always escalates.** Seven of ten unsafe autos were unclassifiable tweets the agent answered anyway. The rule cost five benign jokes a human look and took unsafe autos from 10 to 3. Both runs are kept in `results/` so the change is visible rather than folded into the headline.
17. **The chat cache is committed; the embedding cache is not.** 3,300 chat responses are 15 MB and make `make eval` free on a fresh clone. The embeddings are 737 MB as JSON and regenerate in about two minutes for a few cents.
18. **The judge agreement study uses the same 20 messages for all three systems.** Paired scoring makes the comparison between systems less noisy than 60 unrelated messages would, at the cost of covering fewer messages.
